# Skintel — Voiceover Scripts & Recording Guide

Timings below are the **actual scene boundaries** compiled into the videos, so the read
lands on the cut rather than drifting across it.

---

## The one rule that matters

**Do not read the on-screen text aloud.** The type already delivers the headline. A VO
that repeats it is the single thing that makes a good video feel like a robot narrating
slides — the viewer reads the line and hears it a beat later, and both land weaker.

The VO's job is to say the *other half*: the type states the claim, the voice gives the
reason. Every line below is written to complement what's on screen, never duplicate it.

Pace target is ~130–140 wpm — slower than conversation. Word counts are budgeted with
breathing room; if you naturally run fast, cut words rather than speeding up.

## V1 — "The culprit reveal" (32s) · ~71 words

| In | Out | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 4.6 | "You've been blaming the wrong product." | *"Most people never find the one that's actually causing it."* |
| 4.6 | 9.4 | "Guess. Cut something out. Wait three weeks." | *"Because guessing takes weeks — and by then you've changed three things."* |
| 9.4 | 15.2 | Journal screen | *"Skintel just asks what you used, and how your skin reacted."* |
| 15.2 | 22.4 | The culprit callout | *"Then it finds the pattern you couldn't see. The one product that keeps showing up first."* |
| 22.4 | 27.4 | Verdict + score pills | *"Every product scored against your skin. Not skin in general."* |
| 27.4 | 32.0 | CTA card | *"Founding access is twenty dollars. Link below."* |

**Delivery:** conversational and a little dry. The 15.2 line is the payoff — land
"couldn't see," then take a real beat before "the one product." Silence sells it.

## Hero — "Live UI" (38s) · ~69 words

The only composition where the interface actually animates rather than being filmed
off a screenshot, so the voice has more room than usual to stay out of the way. Let
the ring draw and the bars flip red in silence — the visuals are carrying those beats.

| In | Out | On screen | Say |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 4.6 | "Your shelf is full of suspects." | *"You already own the answer. It's sitting on a shelf."* |
| 4.6 | 12.6 | Scanline sweeps, rows flag | *"Skintel reads every ingredient in one pass — and knows which ones your skin has reacted to before."* |
| 12.6 | 20.6 | Ring draws, 82 counts up | *"Then it scores the whole product against your profile. Not clean or dirty… yours."* |

**Delivery:** the pause before "yours" is the whole line — it's the differentiator
against every other ingredient checker. Don't rush the bar scene; the red flip at
~24.5s wants a beat of silence around it.

## Which to use, per video

Not one answer for all three — the videos do different jobs.

| Video | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** culprit reveal | your voice | It has the shape of a personal story. A founder telling you what their own app found is the whole credibility of the piece, and that's the one thing TTS cannot supply. |
| **V2** scan → verdict | either | Pure explainer. No personal claim, so a synthetic read costs nothing. |
| **V3** the offer | ElevenLabs | It's an ad, and ads are expected to sound produced. Also the one you'll re-cut most as seat counts change — regenerating a line beats re-recording one. |

The honest tiebreaker: **a synthetic VO that ships beats a recorded one that doesn't.**
If recording means this sits for a week, use ElevenLabs on all three and move on.

## Recording it yourself

Where a human wins, it isn't audio quality — ElevenLabs matches or beats a phone
recording on that. It's that an imperfect, specific voice reads as a person with
something at stake, which is worth more in beauty than polish is.

**Setup — no gear needed:**
- Phone Voice Memos, in the most soft-furnished room you have. A closet with clothes
  in it is genuinely the best booth most people own; a duvet over your head works.
- Phone ~15 cm away and **off to the side**, not straight at your mouth — that kills
  the plosive pops on P and B sounds.
- Airplane mode. Fridge, AC, and fans off.
- Leave 2 seconds of silence at the start and end of every take — I need that room
  tone to clean it up.

**Performance:**
- Record each video's script as **one continuous take**, not scene by scene. Do 3–4
  full takes and keep the best; splicing per-scene reads always sounds stitched.
- Smile slightly on the CTA lines. It's audible.
- Slower than feels right. Everyone rushes their first VO.

## ElevenLabs (if you go synthetic)

ElevenLabs is the best TTS available — on a 20–30 second read it is genuinely hard to
distinguish from a person. The tells are almost never timbre; they're **delivery**:
uniform energy, no breaths, and pacing that never varies. All three are settings
problems, not model problems.

**Settings that matter:**

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Eleven v3, or Multilingual v2 if v3 drifts | v3 reads intent better |
| Voice | warm, mid-to-low, conversational | avoid anything labelled "narration" or "promo" — those are announcer voices |
| Stability | ~45–55 | lower is expressive but wanders; higher goes flat |
| Similarity | ~75 | |
| **Style exaggeration** | **0, or near it** | this is the big one — style is what produces the "AI advert voice" |
| Speed | slightly below default | |

**Direct the read with punctuation.** The model has no other way to know your intent:
- `…` gives a real pause — use it where the script says to take a beat
- A full stop instead of a comma shortens the phrase and adds weight
- Splitting one sentence into two short ones slows the whole line down

**Check the domain.** TTS very often mangles `skinstel.com` — it may read "skintel",
spell it out, or stumble on the `.com`. Generate that line on its own first, and if it's
wrong, write it phonetically (`skin-stel dot com`) until it isn't. This is the one line
that has to be perfectly understood.

**Export** the highest quality available (PCM/WAV on paid tiers, otherwise 192 kbps MP3),
then mux with TTS mode so the audio isn't processed twice:

```bash
TTS=1 ./add-vo.sh out/skintel-v3.mp4 v3-vo.wav out/skintel-v3-vo.mp4
```

TTS output is already clean and evenly levelled. Running the mic repair chain over it
compresses already-compressed speech and flattens the little dynamic range the model
produced — a real part of why AI voiceovers sound like AI voiceovers. `TTS=1` skips
straight to loudness normalisation.

## Cleaning and muxing

Once you have `v1-vo.m4a` (or .wav/.mp3), one command does cleanup + mux:

```bash
./add-vo.sh out/skintel-v1.mp4 v1-vo.m4a out/skintel-v1-vo.mp4
```

The script applies a light broadcast chain — 80 Hz high-pass to kill rumble, de-esser,
gentle compression, and loudness normalisation to **-14 LUFS**, which is what TikTok,
Instagram and YouTube all normalise to. Hitting it yourself means the platforms don't
turn your audio down unpredictably.

To add a music bed under the VO:

```bash
./add-vo.sh out/skintel-v1.mp4 v1-vo.m4a out/skintel-v1-vo.mp4 music.mp3
```

Music is ducked to -18 dB under the voice automatically. Keep beds sparse and
instrumental — anything with a strong beat fights the slow type animations.

## If you'd rather not do VO at all

These were built silent on purpose, and silent + trending audio is still the stronger
play for organic TikTok/Reels reach — the algorithm favours native sounds and most
viewers watch muted with captions on.

The best split: **V1 and V2 silent with trending audio** for organic, **V3 with the VO**
for paid ads, where sound-on viewing and a spoken CTA both convert better.
